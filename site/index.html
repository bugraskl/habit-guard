Your hand is halfway to your mouth. Habit Guard noticed first.

Habit Guard watches your webcam for a hand going to your mouth, mustache, brows or hair, and interrupts the habit within about a second: a sound, a dimmed screen, a spoken phrase or a notification, your choice. It runs on your computer, costs a fraction of a percent of your CPU and never saves a picture.

Set it up once. Then forget it.

A hand has to stay in a zone for a moment before anything happens, so drinking, wiping your lips or a quick scratch are ignored.

  1. 1

    Place the camera

    Where your face and both raised hands are in the picture, with light on your face. The camera preview shows exactly what Habit Guard sees.

  2. 2

    Choose your habits

    Tick the ones to watch. Each has its own zone on your face, its own dwell time and size. Zones follow you when you lean in or tilt your head.

  3. 3

    Carry on

    It waits in the tray. When a fingertip stays in a zone for the dwell time the alarm goes off, and steps up while the hand stays.

See what you get

These are the real windows. The camera picture in the preview is an illustration, not a photograph: no real face appears anywhere in this project.

Pause when you need to

Eating, a video call, a phone call with your chin on your hand: pause tracking from the tray for 15 minutes, an hour or three hours, and it resumes by itself. Pausing releases the camera, so the webcam light goes out.

Try the alarm any time with Test the alarm: it plays exactly what your settings describe.

Four habits, four zones

Each zone is a few ellipses on your face, measured in eye distances, so they follow you as you lean in, lean back or tilt your head. The picture is drawn from the same numbers the app uses.

  • Nail and finger biting

    The lips and the area around them.

    1.0 s dwell
  • Mustache, beard and lip picking

    Between the nose and the mouth. Optionally the chin and the beard line.

    1.5 s dwell
  • Brow, lash and hair pulling

    Brows, eyelids, forehead and hairline. Optionally the scalp.

    1.5 s · off
  • Face touching and skin picking

    The rest of the face, whatever the other habits do not own.

    4 s · off

Nail biting and mustache are on by default; the other two are one tick away. Sizes, dwell times and the wider areas are all settings. See zones and tuning.

What happens when it catches you

Every response is a setting. Use one, or all of them together.

  • Sound

    A soft chime, then three beeps, then a warbling alarm as you keep going. Or your own sound file (WAV, MP3, ...).

    On
  • Screen curtain

    Dim the screen or pulse a red frame on every monitor, until the hand is down.

    Dim the screen
  • Voice

    Says a phrase of your choice with your system's offline voice.

    Off
  • Notification

    A desktop notification on the first alarm of each episode.

    On
  • Escalation

    Quieter first, louder and darker the longer the hand stays.

    Every 4 s
  • Pause from the tray

    For 15 minutes, an hour or three hours. It resumes by itself.

    15 m1 h3 h
  • Statistics

    Alarms per day and habit, a 7-day chart and a clean streak, stored locally as plain numbers.

    On
  • Performance profile

    An adaptive analysis rate and a motion gate keep the CPU near a third of a percent.

    Balanced

All of these, with their ranges, are in the configuration reference.

A camera app you can actually trust

Habit Guard watches your face and hands, so privacy is designed in, and anyone can check it.

We removed MediaPipe's runtime because it phones home

The hand models come from Google's MediaPipe (Apache-2.0), but its runtime contains a usage logger that connects to Google's servers. Habit Guard runs the same weights through OpenCV instead, and the runtime is not shipped.

Every change is scanned for networking code and picture writing
  • Network access

    No telemetry, no accounts, no update check, no crash reports. CI fails when any file imports a networking library.

    None
  • Camera pictures

    Analysed in memory for a few milliseconds and never written to disk. CI fails on OpenCV's image and video writers.

    Never stored
  • What is stored

    Your settings and the daily counters, in plain JSON you can read. No images.

    Numbers only
  • Pause

    The camera device is released, so the webcam light goes out.

    Camera off
  • Screen, keyboard, mouse

    The curtain draws over the screen; it never reads it. Keys and clicks are never read.

    Never read

Prefer to run from source?

You need Git and uv, which fetches the right Python itself. Intel Macs use this route too.

  1. A hand icon appears in the tray (the menu bar on macOS). The settings window opens the first time.
  2. Pick the habits to watch, then open Camera preview from the tray menu to check that it sees you well.
  3. Optional: uv run habit-guard autostart enable starts it at login.
git clone https://github.com/bugraskl/habit-guard.git
cd habit-guard
uv sync
uv run habit-guard

Questions people ask

Does it record me?

No. A camera picture exists only in memory for a few milliseconds while it is analysed. Nothing is written to disk or sent anywhere, and a scan in CI fails the build if code that could do either is added.

It goes off when I drink, or talk on the phone. What can I do?

Give that habit a longer dwell time, make its zone smaller, or pause tracking from the tray for a while. The camera preview shows exactly which zone your hand is in.

Does it work with glasses?

Yes. Strong reflections on the lenses can hide the eyes, which are used to place the zones; tilt the lamp or the camera slightly if that happens.

Can I use it during a video call?

Most webcams can be opened by one program at a time. If a call app holds the camera, Habit Guard waits and starts again when the camera is free; you can also pause it from the tray for a set time.

Which webcam do I need?

Any webcam, built-in or external. Place it so that your face and both raised hands are in the picture, with light on your face.

Is it a medical device?

No. It is a self-help tool. If a habit hurts you or causes distress, talking to a doctor or therapist is worth it; many people find reminders like this a helpful addition, not a replacement.

My habit is not on the list.

Zones are just shapes on the face, so new habits are mostly a matter of drawing one. Open a feature request and describe where your hand goes.
